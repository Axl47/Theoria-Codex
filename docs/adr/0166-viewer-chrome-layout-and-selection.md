# 0166. Viewer chrome composes in stages, floating controls follow the footer, and the selected media drives layout

Status: Accepted

## Context

Showing chrome over playing video must not cost one long frame. The footer fades in with the chrome, floating buttons must not jump with it, and several effects must stay in step with the selected post and page while swipes, scrubs and feed updates move underneath them.

## Decision

- `rememberViewerChromeStage`: chrome parts arrive one frame apart, so showing costs several short frames instead of one long one while video plays. Hiding is immediate: `ViewerChromeVisibility` fades in but has no fade-out, so every part it wraps leaves in the next frame, whatever hides it (a tap, a scrub dimming the backdrop, the overview opening), and a hide during a fade-in does not wait for it. Fading all chrome out at once starts from an idle GPU, and on the phone that left about a third of hides presenting one vsync late; without the fade-out the overrun P90 matched v1.9.2 again (3.5-3.8 ms against 8.7-9.2 ms).
- `ViewerFooterButton` floats `VIEWER_FOOTER_BUTTON_GAP` above the footer the current media shows and glides over the same span as the footer's fade between the footer's edge and the screen's edge, instead of jumping. `viewerFooterHeight` is shared with subtitle bottom clearance (`LocalViewerSubtitleBottomLift`). That local and `LocalViewerTimelineInset` change with every toggle, so they are dynamic composition locals: a static one would recompose everything under its provider. The dock's inset already follows the chrome; the timeline or page scrubber and the row above it hide with it.
- In landscape, outside episodes, the footer buttons sit in the post strip's row, level with its thumbnails (`VIEWER_POST_STRIP_CELL_CENTER`), so they no longer cover the media: the rotate chip in a `VIEWER_STRIP_ROW_BUTTON_SLOT` at the start, slideshow and the rotation lock (`VIEWER_STRIP_ROW_BUTTON_WIDTH` more) at the end. The strip keeps those slots even while a button is away, so it never shifts. They appear with the strip's chrome stage, and with the chrome hidden they fall back to the footer-less position, where the rotate chip's hint shows. Portrait and the episode row keep the floating placement.
- While scrubbing, everything but the timeline steps back through alpha only, so the strip keeps its scroll position (`scrubFade`). A hold-scrub needs the timeline on screen: `beginScrub` records whether it had to show hidden chrome, and `endScrub` hides by value (`SetChromeVisible(false)`) rather than toggling, because begin and end can share one frame's controls whose visibility is stale. Chrome a hold-scrub shows only for its timeline stays at alpha zero from its first frame (`rememberViewerScrubFade` reads `chromeShownForScrub` in its layer, and the footer controls and watch-mode backdrop step back too), because the timeline only reports the scrub once it has been laid out, a few frames after the reveal. `markPassiveInteraction` keeps open controls alive for seeking, zooming and navigation but never opens them; only a tap does.
- A page a swipe heads to shows its timeline too, because the swipe only selects it once the slide settles. Trail forks leave when a swipe heads to another post, since the selection changes only once the swipe lands and a fork returning during the slide would open over the post being left. A caught swipe commits while its pages are still moving, so only a resting pager follows selection. Selecting another page already closes the overview in the reducer; a feed page arriving must not.
- `ViewerFrame` and `ViewerControls` (`rememberViewerFrame`, `rememberViewerControls`) stay the same objects across a chrome toggle and read visibility through a `State`, so a toggle recomposes only what reads it; `ViewerFrame.uiState` always reads as visible, and readers use `ViewerFrame.chromeVisible`. Any other change replaces them, since an instance held across a change would toggle chrome or dismiss against a passed state. Pages remember their renderer events and video options so an unchanged page skips recomposition. `ViewerSelection.mediaItems` is keyed by post so a readiness change rebuilds neither the media list nor its kinds. A new slideshow returns zoomed media to fit so the next touch reaches the gesture that stops it.
- `systemControlsReady` is false while the Viewer opens (connecting system controls costs frames) and once its session ended, because a closing Viewer's paused pages stay for the return transition and a system Play would start the closed Viewer's player.
- Layout of motion media: still images always fill the page; animated and video media sit under a 40 dp portrait top gap clear of the top bar unless the layout is edge to edge (landscape, picture-in-picture or reading a chapter). A post whose motion resolves only after opening (`Post.awaitsMotion`) keeps the gap while it shows its poster, so it does not shift when the stream arrives.
- `rememberViewerTouchExploration` tracks accessibility-service changes while the Viewer is open without making playback depend on accessibility.
- Accent: `viewerAccentFromPixels` samples the thumbnail at 48 px so small vivid details survive downscaling, buckets hues, weights vividness over area, discounts skin hues (10-50 degrees, saturation below 0.7, weight 0.15) because they would give nearly every character art the same peach, returns null (white accent) when fewer than 6% of pixels are colourful (monochrome art such as manga), then fixes saturation and lightness so the accent reads over dark scrims. Sampling waits for `enabled` so it never competes with the opening animation, and swiping past a post cancels its sample.
- Accent blending: `LocalViewerAccent` is a `ColorProducer` that never changes (`ViewerAccentState`), and its readers call it only while drawing (`ViewerAccentIcon`, `viewerAccentBorder`, canvases, `BasicText`'s colour), so a swipe redraws the accent and recomposes nothing. It blends the tints of the two posts either side of the post pager's position, so it follows a swipe, a caught swipe, a cancelled one and the strip's or a chapter's animated page changes alike. Once a post settles, and again when posts are added beside it, its neighbours are sampled from the image cache only (`cachedOnly`, no network), so a tint is known only for media already fetched, whatever the source; a neighbour still unknown shows the settled tint, and one arriving mid-swipe fades in over `SHORT_MS`. A new selection starts from the colour its post already shows and tweens to its tint over `MEDIUM_MS`: when the pager has reached the post (a swipe commits where it lands, a caught one mid-way) that colour is the post's own blend share, so the commit changes nothing on screen; otherwise, as when a slideshow step jumps the pager, it is the blended colour on screen, or the last settled colour when the pager was replaced with a new session. A tint sampled after its post settled tweens the same way. A derived state keeps the readers from redrawing while the blended colour stays the same.

## Consequences

New chrome parts need a stage. The footer-height helper is the single source for anything that must stay clear of the bottom stack.
