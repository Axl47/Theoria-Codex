ExecPlan: Media Buffering, Seeking and Gallery Paging

Measure and reduce what the Viewer and feed spend on video (time to first frame, rebuffering, bytes, duplicate bytes), seeking (seek-to-resume and scrub preview frames), and many-page galleries (open time, per-page load, prefetch, memory). Round 1 built a repeatable on-device benchmark, recorded a baseline, and landed five measured fixes. Round 2 bounded the video cache's range locks, sped up the first scrub preview of a drag, capped neighbour video prefetch, and added swipe, scroll and two-drag scenarios. Later rounds push to the same branch and append to the same metrics file.

Purpose / Big Picture

Video and multi-page posts are where the app spends most of its data and where waiting is most visible. The audit in the "Performance Audit of App and CI" thread fixed several media items (A2 metered prefetch cap, A13 memory-only nearest neighbours, A14 offline save from cache, A15 shared ugoira metadata) but never measured buffering, seeking or paging. This plan adds numbers first, then fixes what the numbers show.

After round 1: hidden feed cards stop downloading when the Viewer opens, the Viewer caches the clip it plays instead of re-downloading it, gallery pages a prefetch already downloaded show without a network round trip, the next page is decoded before it is reached, far pages are warmed without full-size decodes, and a gallery's first page no longer shares the link with speculative downloads.

After round 2: feed cards and neighbour prefetch lock at most 2 MB of a stream per request, so a Viewer opened from a feed card and the player of a neighbour still being prefetched read what those writers already downloaded instead of fetching it again. A drag's first preview frame no longer waits 80 ms, and neighbour videos warm only their first 4 MB on every network.

Terms. TTFF: time to first rendered video frame. Seek-to-resume: from a seek request until the player is ready to play at the target. Cache range lock: Media3's SimpleCache lets one writer at a time fill a range; a progressive request locks from its read position to the next cached span or the end of the file (since round 2, feed cards and prefetch lock at most 2 MB), and any other reader of a locked range reads around the cache (downloads without caching). Bytes a request writes become readable when a 5 MB fragment fills or the request ends. Warm (images): the file is in Coil's disk cache. Decoded: the bitmap is in Coil's memory cache.

Provider feeds can contain illegal content. Device work uses logs and generated media only: the benchmark never touches provider media, screenshots or recordings.

Measurement Harness

app/src/androidTest/java/com/theoriacodex/app/viewer/MediaBufferingBenchmarkTest.kt is an opt-in instrumented test (skipped unless -e mediaBench true). It drives the app's real feed and Viewer pools, shared video cache, the page's scrub previews (ViewerScrubPreviews, as the popup drives them), a real ViewerViewModel with the route's prefetcher (viewerMediaPrefetcher) and planner (viewerPrefetchAction), and Coil's loader. BenchMediaServer.kt is a throttled HTTP/1.1 server inside the app process: one shared token bucket (default 16 Mbit/s), 100 ms before each response's headers, ranges, ETag/Last-Modified and 304s but no Cache-Control (an nginx origin's default). It records every byte written per path as an interval, so it reports total, unique and duplicate bytes, including responses still in flight.

scripts/media_bench_device.py run generates media with ffmpeg (6 × 15 s and 3 × 90 s 1080p H.264 at 4 Mbit/s with AAC; 80 JPEG pages of 1280×1808, about 410 KB each), builds and verifies the debug and test APKs (scripts/verify_device_apk.py), copies the media into the debug app's cache/media-bench through run-as, runs each scenario once per run in a fresh process, and writes medians to build/media-bench/<label>/results.json. record appends a round to the metrics file.

ScenarioWhat it does
feedToViewerFour feed cards autoplay together for 5 s; the first opens in the Viewer (cards pause, leases return after the 1 s grace), and the clip loops twice (31 s). Since round 1 it then returns to the feed and requires all four cards to play again, also after a Viewer that closes within the grace.
viewerSeekAndScrubA 90 s video opens cold; four seeks past the buffer; a drag through the page's scrub previews resting on three targets while it plays; a release seek to the last previewed position; after 1.5 s a second drag on one target (round 2). Reports a request timeline for the drags in diagnostics.
viewerSwipeRound 2. Three 90 s videos in one post open on the middle one through a real ViewerViewModel and prefetcher; it plays 20 s (--swipe-watch-ms), then a swipe leases the right neighbour, makes it current after 300 ms and plays it for 3 s. Uses an nhentai-sourced post: a Gelbooru post's media is repaired to https, which the plain-HTTP server cannot answer.
feedScrollRound 2. Four cards autoplay for 2 s; two scroll off (pause, return warm after 300 ms) and two come on; after 8 s they scroll back. Every visible card must autoplay.
galleryPagingAn 80-page nhentai-shaped post: open, read 40 pages (1.2 s after each appears), skim 35 pages (200 ms each). Each page is requested the way its AsyncImage resolves it (page location, screen size, inexact, fit).
Why in-process loopback, not a host server. A first version served from the Mac through adb reverse. adb buffers far more than a phone's TCP window, so abandoned or paused progressive requests looked like 18.6 MB of background traffic where the loopback version shows 10.3 MB. Loopback socket buffers behave much like a phone's receive window.

Phases

Phase 0: Harness and baseline (done)

Commits a9069fac (share the route prefetcher, prefetch queue and scrub frame source so the benchmark runs production code) and 61759ea6 (benchmark). Baseline recorded as round 0 at 61759ea6.

Phase 1: Video – stop feed previews while a Viewer stream plays (done, 51ae87c0)

FindingAt Viewer open, feed players were paused but kept loading toward their buffer target with their connections open. The two warm cards downloaded their whole clips while hidden (10.3 MB). The opened clip's card held its cache range, so the Viewer read around the cache and re-downloaded that range on the next loop (0.8 MB).
ChangeViewerVideoPlayerPool.acquire calls VideoPlaybackInfrastructure.onViewerStreamAcquired, which stops every feed player (FeedPreviewPlayerPool.stopForViewer). Stopped players keep media, position and (keep-content player view) the last frame. A still-leased card resumes through resume; a bound idle slot re-prepares on its next lease.
ConstraintEvery visible feed card still autoplays: the feed is hidden while a Viewer stream exists, and cards re-prepare from the cache when shown.

Phase 2: Images – use cached images without revalidating (done, 1de77259)

FindingAll 41 displayed pages per run came from the network even though prefetch had already downloaded them: Coil revalidated each cached page (a 100 ms round trip, 304) because the origin sent validators but no freshness lifetime. Without validators it re-downloads them (21 MB duplicates in the first trial).
ChangerespectCacheHeaders(false) on the app ImageLoader; UndecodableImageEviction drops the disk entry of any load that fails for a non-network reason, so a bad cached body cannot stick.

Phase 3: Gallery – decode the next page before it is reached (done, cb6b539a)

FindingA page first prefetched while 2–3 away was only downloaded; its warmed state then made it terminal, so it was never decoded into memory as it came close. Only one displayed page per run was a memory hit.
ChangeviewerPrefetchAction marks static images within VIEWER_MEMORY_PREFETCH_DISTANCE as decodeKeys; the reducer re-queues a warmed image once if it is a decode key and not yet decoded; ViewerPrefetchResult.decoded records it.

Phase 4: Gallery – warm far pages without full-size decodes (done, 5ac2058f)

FindingFar neighbours (disk only) were still decoded at their original size, then discarded.
ChangeStatic images warmed for disk only decode subsampled to a 64 px edge; the full file is still written to the disk cache.

Phase 5: Viewer – load the first media before its neighbours (done, 237540b3, refined in 81935f4f)

FindingOpening a gallery started page 1 and two neighbour downloads at once on a shared link.
ChangePrefetch jobs wait until the session's first media is ready (or 1.5 s, or an error). The first version waited on every page; the interleaved run showed skimming dropping to 91% shown, because a page flipped past before it loaded held back the pages after it. Only the first media waits now. The benchmark reports MediaReady after each shown page, as the renderer does (2cf15750).

Phase 6: Viewer – decode downloaded pages outside the download slots (done, 31bad82f)

FindingPhase 3's decode-ahead jobs took one of the two prefetch slots on every page flip, halving download throughput while skimming (77–86% shown in a same-session run).
ChangeViewerViewModel.launchPrefetch runs a prefetch of already-warmed media without a semaphore permit. Skimming returned to 100% shown.

Round 2, Phase 7: Harness – drags, swipes and scrolls (done, c0279ac0, 2ef4ca85, 42ce3d2b, 3b6d104d, d948d64a, da41f33d)

The timeline remembers one ViewerScrubPreviews per Viewer video page and each drag's popup follows it, so the benchmark drags through the same code (behaviour unchanged in that commit). New scenarios viewerSwipe and feedScroll, a second drag in viewerSeekAndScrub, and a per-request server timeline. The round 1 comparison build is 2ef4ca85: round 1's head plus only these benchmark commits.

Round 2, Phase 8: Video – feed cards and prefetch lock at most 2 MB per cache request (done, 9ccba58b, narrowed in 1bf1baff)

FindingA progressive request locked the cache from its position to the end of the file, so other readers ahead of it read around the cache: the Viewer behind a feed card (0.33–0.46 MB re-downloaded), and the swiped-to player of a neighbour whose prefetch was still running. Its prefetched bytes sat in the writer's open 5 MB fragment, so the player re-downloaded them (swipe after 8 s: 622 ms to first frame with 4.9 MB already prefetched, n=1).
ChangeBoundedLockCache caps each cache hole at 2 MB; CacheDataSource commits what a request wrote and opens the next. RangeRecordingDataSource records a file's length from its first ranged response. The last stretch of a file is read unbounded, so no request starts at its end (no 416 loop); a server that ignores ranges turns bounding off for that file (one extra chunk downloaded once). Fully read files keep their length and bytes, so offline saves from the cache still copy whole files (BoundedVideoCacheTest).
NarrowedThe first version bounded every reader. Interleaved runs showed it cost scrub previews past the buffer about 150 ms (round 1 build 467–503 ms, bounded 616–652 ms): the Viewer player's request ended every 2 MB, a preview's 1 s read-ahead ran into the chunk the player was still writing, downloaded around it on a second connection, and the next preview first spent about 100 ms closing that connection. In the benchmark, round 1's player lock was already bounded by cached islands from earlier seeks, so bounding it gained nothing there. 1bf1baff bounds only feed cards and prefetch; the Viewer's player and previews read as before.
Not changedSeek after scrub: the preview shows the closest keyframe (often after the finger), while the release seek is exact and decodes from the keyframe before it, so it needs bytes the preview never fetched. Landing the release on the previewed frame would make it a cache hit but changes where playback lands (product decision, see opportunities).

Round 2, Phase 9: Scrub – a drag's first frame without the settle wait (done, 38fddddf)

FindingEvery preview frame waited 80 ms to see whether the finger kept moving, including the first, where it just landed. The request timeline shows the rest of a first frame: about 340 ms of extractor setup before its request (new player, decoder and GL pipeline, and a decode at 0:00 that FrameExtractor always does first), 100 ms to first byte, then transfer and decode. Each later target: about 100 ms closing the previous response (Android's HTTP client drains it for up to 100 ms), then the same request path.
ChangefollowScrubTargets extracts the first target at once; later targets still wait 80 ms of rest.
Tried and removedHolding the Viewer player's loading during a drag (a LoadControl wrapper) so previews get the link: no measurable gain, because the player's in-flight 2 MB request keeps arriving into socket buffers after loading pauses. Reusing one extractor across drags: Media3's FrameExtractor is a process singleton that refuses a request while a cancelled one is still decoding ("Another task is already active"), and its idle player would hold a request open; estimated gain about 150 ms on second and later drags only.

Round 2, Phase 10: Viewer – warm only the opening seconds of neighbour videos (done, 8a8fe592)

FindingOn unmetered networks each neighbour video warmed up to 24 MB (metered: 4 MB). In viewerSwipe the unvisited neighbour cost 12–14 MB in 20 s.
ChangeEvery network warms the first 4 MB (VIEWER_VIDEO_PREFETCH_BYTES). Phase 8 lets the swiped-to player read a prefetch that is still running, so a swipe still starts from the cache.

Round 2: hidden feed players while scrolling (measured, not changed)

feedScroll shows two cards scrolled off after 2 s on screen download about 0.25 MB each in the next 8 s (their 2 MB buffer target is nearly met by then), not the 2–3 MB per clip round 1 estimated from the Viewer case. Too small to justify changing warm-player behaviour.

Surprises And Discoveries

  • Paused progressive players keep loading toward their buffer target with an open connection; the server keeps writing into socket buffers beyond what the player reads (about 2.3 MB per paused clip on loopback). Cached bytes after the run (5.2 MB of a 7.6 MB clip sent) showed the gap.
  • Media3 CacheDataSource reads around (without caching) any range another writer holds, re-checking every 100 KB. A paused feed player of the same clip, or a playing Viewer player ahead of a scrub preview, therefore makes the other reader's bytes uncacheable.
  • Scrub preview frames past the buffer cost about 0.9 MB each and do not warm the release seek, for that reason (the Viewer player's open request locks the rest of the file).
  • Coil 2 reports a revalidated (304) cached image as a network load; a revalidation per displayed page was invisible in the app but cost a full round trip.
  • Media3 1.11.1 defaults are already 1 s to start and 2 s after a rebuffer; the Viewer uses them.
  • Benchmark sessions drift: unchanged seek code measured 538 ms and 625 ms in two sessions, and skim shown ranged 77–91% for one build across sessions. Compare builds interleaved in one session.
  • Prefetch scheduling interacts with skimming in ways unit tests do not show: both "wait for the page on screen" and "decode in a download slot" were harmless while reading and harmful while skimming.
  • Round 2: a running prefetch made its own bytes useless to a swipe. CacheDataSink commits a span only when its 5 MB fragment fills or the request closes, and the writer's lock covered the rest of the file, so a player swiping to a neighbour with 4.9 MB prefetched downloaded it all again.
  • Round 2: a Gelbooru post's media is repaired to https when the Viewer loads it, so the first swipe scenario's prefetches hung in a TLS handshake against the plain-HTTP bench server. A thread dump from inside the test found it.
  • Round 2: Kotlin interface delegation (by) does not forward Java default methods. A delegating LoadControl crashed at player creation, because its defaults fall back to deprecated methods that throw. Forward such interfaces explicitly and test the forwarding.
  • Round 2: FrameExtractor always prepares at 0:00 and decodes that frame before seeking to the requested one; when the start of the file is not cached, a drag's first preview also downloads about 1 MB there.
  • Round 2: closing a partly read HTTP response on Android drains it for up to 100 ms before dropping the connection, and every seek and preview move closes one.
  • Round 2: the scrub scenario's earlier seeks leave cached islands that bound the playing request's lock, so it cannot show the "open a video and scrub ahead" case where the lock reaches the end of the file.
  • Round 2: in interleaved pairs the second variant was consistently a little slower (feed first frame, gallery skimming 91–97% vs 100% on unchanged gallery code); a reversed session moved the penalty to the other build.

Outcomes And Retrospective

Round 1 removed waste rather than tuning buffers. The two biggest effects were invisible in the app: hidden feed players downloading whole clips behind the Viewer, and a network round trip for every cached gallery page. Both came from defaults behaving as designed (a paused progressive player keeps its request open; Coil revalidates responses without a freshness lifetime). The measurements caught a regression in my own first version of "first media alone" (skimming fell to 77–91% shown) that unit tests could not have found; the fix restored 100%. Seeking and scrub previews are network-bound and limited by Media3's cache range locks; they need the structural change listed first below.

Round 2: bounded cache locks paid off where two readers share a stream (Viewer from a feed card 438 → 264 ms to playing, swipe after 8 s 618 → 354 ms), but not where round 1 expected: the scrub scenario's earlier seeks had already bounded the playing request's lock, and bounding the Viewer's own player made previews slower. The first interleaved session caught that, and request timelines explained it, so the bounding was narrowed to the writers whose locks others hit. Two smaller lessons: the variant measured second in each pair is slightly slower (swap the order), and a single 4-seek median flips between two modes, so judge seeks on pooled samples. Scrub previews moved (first frame 904 → 737 ms, second drag 811 → 662 ms) mostly from skipping the settle wait; what remains is FrameExtractor's setup and the HTTP drain, both listed first below.

Remaining Opportunities (ranked)

  1. Scrub preview player that starts at the target (high for scrubbing, medium-high effort). Media3's FrameExtractor builds a player, decoder and GL pipeline per drag and always decodes 0:00 before seeking: about 340 ms of each drag's first frame (737 ms now), plus about 1 MB downloaded when the start of the file is not cached. A page-level hidden ExoPlayer rendering to an ImageReader, prepared at the first target and kept across drags (closing its request when a drag ends), could bring first frames to about 350–400 ms and second drags lower. Risk: device-specific surface and format handling.
  2. Close abandoned HTTP responses without draining them (medium-high for seeks and previews, medium effort). Every seek and every preview move past the buffer closes a partly read response, which Android's HttpURLConnection drains for up to 100 ms first. An HTTP source that cancels instead (for example Media3's OkHttpDataSource on the app's OkHttp client, with an interceptor that cancels unfinished bodies) would save about 100 ms per seek (seek to resume about 540–640 ms now) and per later preview frame, and brings HTTP/2 connection reuse. Needs care with provider headers and cookies.
  3. Release on the previewed frame (high for the release seek, product decision). The preview shows the closest keyframe; the release seeks exactly to the finger and decodes from the keyframe before it, needing bytes the preview never fetched (seek after scrub about 460–500 ms). Seeking to the previewed frame's time would make it a cache hit (estimated 150–200 ms) and land where the preview showed, up to half a keyframe interval from the finger.
  4. Shorter start buffer after a seek (medium, robustness call). Media3 waits for 1 s buffered after a seek; 0.5 s would save about 125 ms per seek at 16 Mbit/s but risks a stall on links near the video's bitrate.
  5. Instant seek mode (medium, opt-in setting). With "instant seek" on, every drag movement issues an exact seek on the main player. Media3 1.11's setScrubbingModeEnabled is designed for this. Needs a scenario of rapid seeks.
  6. Gallery memory (medium for large Pixiv pages, product decision). Pages next to the current one are decoded at original size (sharp pinch zoom). Decoding at screen size, or 2× screen size, would cut peak memory for large originals; nhentai pages are already near screen size.
  7. Gallery window shape (low on this link). Skimming is already 100% at 16 Mbit/s. A forward-biased window (more ahead, fewer behind) and a third download slot only matter on slower links or larger pages; test at 4–8 Mbit/s before changing.
  8. Hidden feed players while scrolling (low). Measured in round 2: about 0.25 MB per scrolled-past clip, not 2–3 MB.

Idempotence And Recovery

  • The runner is safe to rerun: media generation and the device copy are skipped when unchanged (manifest of names and sizes); each run uses fresh URLs, and each scenario removes its video cache entries and gallery disk entries afterwards.
  • Only com.theoriacodex.debug and its test package are installed, after scripts/verify_device_apk.py checks the application ID and debug signing. Production com.theoriacodex is never touched.
  • To remove the benchmark media from the phone: adb -s <serial> shell run-as com.theoriacodex.debug rm -rf cache/media-bench.
  • Each change is one commit; revert any of them independently. Phase 3 changes Viewer state shape (ViewerPrefetchState.decoded, QueuePrefetch.decodeKeys); both have defaults.
