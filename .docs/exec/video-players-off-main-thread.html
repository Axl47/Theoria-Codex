ExecPlan: Measure and reduce video player work on the main thread

Feed video previews are driven from the main thread, the thread that draws the screen. This plan first makes that cost measurable and makes the autoplay check honest, then, only if a phone measurement shows the cost matters, tries the cheapest change that removes the measured part. Moving the players to their own thread is the last and most expensive option, not the starting point.

Purpose / Big Picture

The app plays several short videos at once in its feeds. Each is a Media3 ExoPlayer. A player decodes on its own playback thread, but it is controlled from one "application thread": creating it, preparing it, attaching and detaching the surface it draws into, play and pause, and every event it reports back. In this app that thread is the main thread, because the standard video view (PlayerView) requires it.

The threading audit recorded this as the largest remaining category of non-drawing work on the main thread, and also the least measured. The existing pool already limits the damage: players are reused, and creation, preparation and release are paced so they do not pile into one frame. Whether what remains makes frames late on the phone is not known.

Review of the first draft, which went straight to a "player thread" design, changed the plan in three ways:

  • The slowest call was not being timed. Detaching a player from its surface blocks the calling thread until the playback thread confirms, for up to two seconds in Media3 1.11.1. That happens on the main thread whenever a card is recycled, and no trace section covers it.
  • The autoplay check cannot see a blank card. A card reports "playing" from the player's own flag, which stays true with no surface attached. Any change to surface handling could pass every existing check while showing nothing.
  • The rewrite is much larger than drafted. A custom surface lifecycle has to replace what PlayerView does (cropping, keeping the screen on, first-frame handling, reuse inside the scrolling grid), and the Viewer's player pool reaches into the feed pool in both directions.

So the required outcome of this plan is smaller and comes first: every piece of main-thread player work is visible in a trace, the autoplay check fails on a blank card, and one phone session produces a go or no-go with numbers. Anything after that is conditional.

What to measure, and the decision rule

Signals added in Phase 1

  • Trace sections for all main-thread player work: surface attach and detach, recycle, play and pause, resume, the Viewer's stop of feed previews under its own name, and the Viewer prepare that is missing today. The trace query sums self time so nested sections are not counted twice, and the captures include main-thread call-stack samples so time inside androidx.media3 outside any section is still attributed.
  • An honest "playing" state. A card reports playing only when the player says so and at least one frame has been rendered since the current surface was attached. The benchmarks read the same card description, so they inherit the stricter meaning.
  • Time to first frame per card: from surface attach to the first rendered frame, emitted as a trace slice. This is the regression guard for every later change.
  • A query script in scripts/ that reads a Perfetto trace and reports, per scenario: frames, late frames from the platform's frame timeline, and for each late frame the main-thread self time spent in player work inside that frame and in the gap before it, split into construction and prepare, surface attach and detach, and commands and events.

Decision rule for the phone session (Phase 2)

Three complete runs of the same benchmark APK, on the phone, of mixedMediaFiveFeedJourney and searchConcurrentAutoplayScroll, traces retained. With the frame budget taken from the phone's actual refresh rate:

  • Stop if late frames are under 1% of frames in both scenarios, or if player work takes a quarter of the frame budget or more in fewer than 30% of late frames. The plan closes with the numbers recorded.
  • Otherwise one experiment is authorised: the option below that targets the category with the most time in late frames.

These thresholds are proposals. The user can change them before the session; they are fixed before the data is read.

Rule for keeping an experiment

Against the Phase 2 baseline, same phone, same conditions, three runs each: late frames in the mixed journey fall by at least a quarter; the 95th percentile of time to first frame is no more than 10% worse; the stricter autoplay check and the pixel-motion check pass in every iteration. Otherwise the experiment is reverted and recorded.

Requirements a player-thread design must meet (from review)

  • Surface ownership per view, not per card. One binding object per TextureView owns the listener and every texture, each with a generation number. It answers false to onSurfaceTextureDestroyed and releases the texture itself, exactly once, only after the player has confirmed detach or been released. The main thread never waits for the player thread. A transition table covers: the view moving to another card with its texture still available; a texture destroyed without the view detaching (memory trim); closing during prepare; a detach that times out.
  • Identities. Sessions, leases and surfaces carry generations; commands and events from an old generation are dropped, and surfaces are cleared with the identity-checking clearVideoSurface(surface).
  • One queue is a shared stall. A blocking detach or release holds up every other preview's attach, play and events. The design states this cost, measures queue age, resets the poster when a surface is re-attached, and handles a detach timeout without leaving the card on a blank frame.
  • Viewer integration both ways. Stopping previews for the Viewer becomes asynchronous, so the design defines the order in which previews give up decoders and cache ranges and the Viewer starts, without blocking main. Active counts become atomic counters. resume folds into activation.
  • What replaces PlayerView. A table for each setting in use: cropping to fill computed from video size including pixel aspect ratio, keepScreenOn, first-frame and poster rules, content kept across reset, sizing for an already-playing player.
  • State ownership. Which thread owns each one-shot flag in the card's listener; callbacks into Compose arrive on main; a seek from the position hand-over cannot run before the lease exists.
  • Tests. MediaBufferingBenchmarkTest and ViewerVideoPlayerPoolTest call the pool on the main looper and must be reworked; interleaving tests cover two sessions and one reused player. The first step posts through a handler on main, so ordering bugs appear before the thread moves.

Out of scope in every option: the full-screen Viewer player (media session, picture-in-picture) and the scrub preview player.
