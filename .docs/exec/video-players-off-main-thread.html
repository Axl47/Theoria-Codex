ExecPlan: Measure and reduce video player work on the main thread

Feed video previews are driven from the main thread, the thread that draws the screen. This plan first makes that cost measurable, then, only if a phone measurement shows the cost matters, tries the cheapest change that removes the measured part. Moving the players to their own thread is the last and most expensive option, not the starting point.

Purpose / Big Picture

The app plays several short videos at once in its feeds. Each is a Media3 ExoPlayer. A player decodes on its own playback thread, but it is controlled from one "application thread": creating it, preparing it, attaching and detaching the surface it draws into, play and pause, and every event it reports back. In this app that thread is the main thread, because the standard video view (PlayerView) requires it.

The threading audit recorded this as the largest remaining category of non-drawing work on the main thread, and also the least measured. The existing pool already limits the damage: players are reused, and creation, preparation and release are paced so they do not pile into one frame. Whether what remains makes frames late on the phone is not known.

Two review rounds changed the plan:

  • The slowest call is not timed. Detaching a player from its surface, or replacing the surface, blocks the calling thread until the playback thread confirms, for up to two seconds in Media3 1.11.1. That happens on the main thread whenever a card is recycled, and no trace section covers it.
  • A player thread is a much larger rewrite than first drafted. A custom surface lifecycle has to replace what PlayerView does, and the Viewer's player pool reaches into the feed pool in both directions.
  • The decision needs exact definitions. "Late frame", "player work in a frame" and "better" each had more than one reading, and different readings give different answers.

So the required outcome is small and comes first: main-thread player work is visible in a trace, a script turns a trace into the numbers the decision uses, and one phone session produces stop, go or inconclusive. Anything after that is conditional.

Media3 1.11.1 facts established in review from the published classes:

  • PlayerView.setPlayer requires the main thread and a main-thread player. A player may be given another application thread with ExoPlayer.Builder.setLooper, and may be constructed on a different thread from it.
  • Clearing a surface, or replacing it with a different one, waits for the playback thread up to DEFAULT_DETACH_SURFACE_TIMEOUT_MS (2,000 ms), and a timeout becomes a player error. PlayerView.switchTargetView replaces the surface, so it waits too. Attaching the first surface does not wait. release() waits up to 500 ms.
  • onRenderedFirstFrame fires again after a new surface is attached. The listener receives onSurfaceSizeChanged(0, 0) when the view's texture is destroyed or cleared, but no size event when a surface is replaced by one of the same size.

What to measure

Trace sections (measured time)

A trace section records when a piece of code started and ended on its thread, including time it spent blocked. Phase 1 adds sections so that every call the app makes into a player on the main thread sits inside one, in four categories:

CategorySections
ConstructionThe existing create section; PlayerView inflation under its own name, so it is not counted as surface work. Names: TheoriaPreviewPlayerCreate, TheoriaPreviewViewInflate.
PrepareThe existing prepare and rebind sections, and the hand-over seek (:169 and FeedVideoPositionHandoff.publish, both through the seek the card binds). Names: TheoriaPreviewPrepare, TheoriaPreviewPlayerPrepare, TheoriaPreviewPlayerRebind, TheoriaPreviewHandoffSeek.
SurfaceAssigning or clearing playerView.player at :256, :305, :311, :315 and in the factory and update blocks; only when the assignment actually changes the player. Names: TheoriaPreviewSurfaceAttach (any non-null player, including a replacement), TheoriaPreviewSurfaceDetach (clearing).
Commands and eventsPlay and pause (:189-194); pool recycle, resume, cool and release; the Viewer's stop of feed previews under its own name; the bodies of the card's own player-listener callbacks. Names: TheoriaPreviewPlayPause, TheoriaPreviewPlayerRecycle, TheoriaPreviewPlayerResume, TheoriaPreviewPlayerCool, TheoriaPreviewPlayerRelease, TheoriaPreviewStopForViewer, TheoriaPreviewListenerBind (the card adding or removing its listener and reading the ready duration), TheoriaPreviewPlayerEvent.

The script uses each section's self time (its duration minus the sections nested inside it), so nothing is counted twice.

What sections cannot see (estimated time)

Three kinds of main-thread player work happen inside library code the app cannot wrap: a surface attached later from the texture's "available" callback during drawing; a detach caused by the texture being destroyed; and Media3 delivering events before it reaches the app's listeners. To size these, one separate capture samples the main thread's call stack. Inside late frames, it counts the samples with a Media3 frame on the stack that fall outside every section, and multiplies by the sampling period to estimate untraced player time. Samples taken while a Viewer player is active are left out, because a stack cannot say which player they serve. The same trace's section self time inside late frames is the comparison. Sampling fires only while the thread runs, so it cannot see blocked time; sections can. This is an estimate, it adds load, and it is never mixed into the decision numbers. Its only use is the "inconclusive" check below. The script also prints the share of late-frame samples that are player work; that share leaves out blocked waits, so it is context and does not decide anything.

Time to first frame per presentation

A presentation is one card showing one video from the moment it asks the pool for a player until it shows a frame or goes away. Each gets two trace slices that can span many main-thread messages (asynchronous slices, one identifier per presentation):

  • TheoriaPreviewDemandToFirstFrame: from the lease request to the first-frame callback. This is the guard: it includes waiting for a player, construction and prepare, so a change that only moves delay earlier cannot improve it.
  • TheoriaPreviewAttachToFirstFrame: from surface attach to the first-frame callback. Diagnostic only.

The end point is the callback on the main thread, which is when the card removes its poster, so it matches what the user sees. A presentation that ends without a frame is recorded as abandoned with its duration, and the script reports abandoned presentations instead of leaving them out. The names are new because the benchmarks already count TheoriaPreviewFirstFrame.

The query script

scripts/trace_player_frames.py reads one Perfetto trace and prints, per scenario, the quantities defined in the next section. Its definitions:

  • Frame: one row per frame of the benchmark app's own process in the platform's frame timeline (actual_frame_timeline_slice), one per frame token; if a token appears for more than one layer, the longest row is used.
  • Late frame: a frame whose jank type includes an application deadline miss. Frames whose only jank types are compositor, display, prediction or buffer-stuffing causes are counted separately and are not "late" here, because main-thread work in the app cannot have caused them.
  • The frame's main-thread interval: the frame is joined by its token to the main thread's Choreographer#doFrame <token> slice. The interval ends when that slice ends. It starts at the frame's expected start, or at the end of the previous doFrame slice if that is later. Work before the expected start cannot have delayed this frame; work between frames after it can. Intervals do not overlap, so each millisecond of section time belongs to at most one frame.
  • Frame budget: the frame's own expected duration from the timeline, so a display that changes refresh rate is handled.
  • Player-heavy late frame: a late frame whose interval contains player section self time of at least a quarter of its budget.

The script separates reading the trace from the attribution arithmetic, and the arithmetic has a unit test on hand-written rows: nested sections, a section spanning two frames, a section before the expected start, a frame with two layers.

Capture setup

  • Decision runs use macrobenchmark's default trace capture, unsampled. Phase 1's first step confirms on the emulator that this capture contains the frame timeline, the app's own sections and the doFrame slices; if anything is missing, the plan records the exact capture configuration that adds it before going further.
  • The sampled run is taken outside macrobenchmark with a written Perfetto configuration (CPU call-stack sampling of the benchmark process, which is profileable), kept in the script's header. The benchmark build is minified, so its R8 mapping file is kept next to the APK and used to name the samples.
  • On the emulator, macrobenchmark needs the instrumentation argument androidx.benchmark.suppressErrors=EMULATOR. It is passed on the command line for emulator runs only and is not committed to the build.
  • Confirmed in Phase 1 (emulator, androidx.benchmark 1.5.0): the default capture's config, read back from the trace's trace_config_pbtxt, has android.surfaceflinger.frametimeline, atrace_apps: "*" (the app's synchronous and asynchronous sections) and the view atrace category (Choreographer#doFrame <token>). Nothing had to be added. The benchmark fixture runs in a second process, com.theoriacodex.benchmark:benchmarkFixture, so the script reads the package's process and its :name processes, joining frames to doFrame slices within each process, and the sampled configuration matches com.theoriacodex.benchmark*.
  • Offline use: the script takes the path to a trace_processor_shell binary and names the version it was written against, so it does not download anything at run time.

Phase 2: stop, go or inconclusive

Three complete runs of the same benchmark APK on the phone, of mixedMediaFiveFeedJourney and searchConcurrentAutoplayScroll, unsampled, traces retained. For each scenario and each run, over all of that run's iterations:

  • late rate = late frames ÷ frames;
  • player share = player-heavy late frames ÷ late frames.

The value for a scenario is the median of its three runs.

OutcomeConditionWhat follows
InconclusiveIn either scenario the three late rates differ by more than a factor of two, or the sampled run's untraced estimate inside late frames is larger than the section self time inside late frames of that same run.No experiment is authorised. The report says what is missing and names the smallest instrumentation that would resolve it, with its cost, as a recommendation. The user allows a round of instrumentation where it is warranted, but it is not started without the orchestrator.
StopLate rate under 1% in both scenarios, or player share under 30% in both.The plan closes with the numbers recorded.
GoNeither of the above.One experiment: the option whose category holds the most section time inside late frames.

Which measure decides. Stop and go use only the unsampled runs' section measures. Sections record wall time, including the main thread's blocked waits for the playback thread, and those waits are the largest player cost measured so far. The sampled run can only make the outcome inconclusive. It does that when its untraced player time in late frames exceeds the time the sections hold there, meaning the sections miss most of the work (ratio: 1). Both sides of that check are time inside the same run's late frames. The earlier version compared Media3 samples inside sections with Media3 samples outside them. That made it inconclusive by construction: sampling cannot see blocked waits, and "inside" left out non-Media3 work such as view inflation. On "go", the section categories still choose the option, and the untraced estimate is reported beside them.

A player-heavy late frame shows that player work was on the main thread while the frame ran late. It does not prove the frame would have been on time without it. That is why "go" authorises an experiment that is then measured, not a redesign.

Phase 3: keep or revert an experiment

The experiment sits behind a build-time switch. One phone session runs the switch-off and switch-on builds alternately, three runs each, so drift in the device affects both sides. The experiment is kept only if all of these hold:

  • Mixed journey: every switch-on run has a lower late rate than every switch-off run, and the median falls by at least a quarter.
  • Search: the median late rate is no more than 10% worse. Search waits for cards to play inside its measured block, so a change in start-up speed changes how many frames it measures; rates are compared, and if the measured frame count differs by more than 20% between the sides the Search comparison is inconclusive.
  • First frame: in both scenarios the 95th percentile of demand-to-first-frame is no more than 10% worse, and there are no more abandoned presentations lasting over two seconds than before.
  • Autoplay: the mixed journey's pixel-motion check passes in every iteration of every run.

Anything else, including an inconclusive comparison, means the experiment is reverted and recorded. Three runs per side cannot show a small improvement; the rule only accepts a difference large enough that the two sides do not overlap.

What the autoplay evidence does and does not show

The pixel-motion check proves each visible preview changed its pixels at least twice within its retry window. It does not prove continuous playback during a scroll, and a card that renders one frame and then freezes while its player still reports playing would pass the Search scenario. Options A and the pool-side changes do not touch surfaces, so this limit is acceptable for them. An experiment that changes surface handling (option B or C) has an extra entry requirement, below.

What the untraced work implies

The Media3 work the sections cannot wrap is mostly Media3 delivering player events on the player's application thread (ExoPlayerImpl.updatePlaybackInfo, ListenerSet.flushEvents, analytics listeners), plus PlayerView's own listener updating its views (updateForCurrentTrackSelections).

  • A keeps the main thread as the application thread, so this work stays; A removes construction only.
  • B also keeps it. B removes the surface detach on recycling, the largest section cost measured on the emulator, which is almost entirely a blocked wait. A player release still blocks, and a texture the grid destroys still detaches inside the library's callback, which no section sees.
  • C is the only option that moves Media3's event delivery off the main thread. It also has to replace PlayerView, because PlayerView.setPlayer rejects a player whose application thread is not the main thread (Media3 1.11.1). updateForCurrentTrackSelections therefore goes away under C, and whatever replaces it on the main thread is that design's own cost.
  • Under every option, platform diagnostics are on for preview players (VideoPlaybackInfrastructure.playerBuilder does not turn them off). Their listener, MediaMetricsListener, and its session manager run on the main thread. ExoPlayer.Builder.setUsePlatformDiagnostics(false) would remove that work for previews. The user authorised that change on 2026-11-16 as its own switch, measured separately; another thread implements and measures it, outside this plan's scope.

Entry requirement for any surface-handling experiment (B or C)

Before the experiment, the card's "playing" state becomes "the player says playing and a frame has been rendered on the current surface", so a blank card cannot report playing. The signal, from review:

  • a new flag, separate from the poster's hasRenderedFirstFrame, held in a small state holder that host tests can drive (today the rule lives in private Compose code);
  • set in onRenderedFirstFrame before the early return at :219;
  • reset when the card actually changes playerView.player (assigning the same player is a no-op and must not reset it) and on onSurfaceSizeChanged(0, 0);
  • the playing state is recomputed when either input changes, not only in onIsPlayingChanged.

Tests: reuse of an already-playing player; detach and re-attach; a card that renders one frame and then has its surface cleared (playerView.player?.clearVideoSurface() on main, with playback diagnostics enabled and an MP4 fixture added to the instrumented-test assets) must stop reporting playing. Because the Search scenarios wait on this state inside their measured block, adopting it changes their frame metrics, so the stored Search baselines are re-recorded at that point.

Requirements a player-thread design must meet (from review)

  • Surface ownership per view, not per card. One binding object per TextureView owns the listener and every texture, each with a generation number. It answers false to onSurfaceTextureDestroyed and releases the texture itself, exactly once, only after the player has confirmed detach or been released. The main thread never waits for the player thread. A transition table covers: the view moving to another card with its texture still available; a texture destroyed without the view detaching (memory trim); closing during prepare; a detach that times out.
  • Identities. Sessions, leases and surfaces carry generations; commands and events from an old generation are dropped, and surfaces are cleared with the identity-checking clearVideoSurface(surface).
  • One queue is a shared stall. A blocking detach or release holds up every other preview's attach, play and events. The design states this cost, measures queue age, resets the poster when a surface is re-attached, and handles a detach timeout without leaving the card on a blank frame.
  • Viewer integration both ways. Stopping previews for the Viewer becomes asynchronous, so the design defines the order in which previews give up decoders and cache ranges and the Viewer starts, without blocking main. Active counts become atomic counters. resume folds into activation.
  • What replaces PlayerView. A table for each setting in use: cropping to fill computed from video size including pixel aspect ratio, keepScreenOn, first-frame and poster rules, content kept across reset, sizing for an already-playing player.
  • State ownership. Which thread owns each one-shot flag in the card's listener; callbacks into Compose arrive on main; a seek from the position hand-over cannot run before the lease exists.
  • Tests. MediaBufferingBenchmarkTest and ViewerVideoPlayerPoolTest call the pool on the main looper and must be reworked; interleaving tests cover two sessions and one reused player. The first step posts through a handler on main, so ordering bugs appear before the thread moves.

Out of scope in every option: the full-screen Viewer player (media session, picture-in-picture) and the scrub preview player.

  • Decision: The plan's required outcome is measurement; redesign is conditional. Rationale: both reviews showed the first draft could approve a large rewrite without evidence of benefit. Date/Author: 2026-11-16 / Claude.
  • Decision: Try cheaper options before a player thread, chosen by where late-frame time is. Rationale: construction and surface costs each have a smaller fix. Date/Author: 2026-11-16 / Claude.
  • Decision: Keep the player-thread option as requirements only. Rationale: specifying a surface lifecycle in detail before knowing it is needed is wasted design; when it is needed it gets its own review. Date/Author: 2026-11-16 / Claude.
  • Decision: The stricter "playing" rule moves out of Phase 1 and becomes the entry requirement for surface-handling experiments. Rationale: the Phase 2 decision does not need it, the mixed journey already checks pixels, and it changes Search baselines. Date/Author: 2026-11-16 / Claude.
  • Decision: The decision uses measured section time from unsampled runs; call-stack sampling is one separate run used only to detect that the sections miss too much. Rationale: sampling changes the load it measures and yields estimates. Date/Author: 2026-11-16 / Claude.
  • Decision: "Inconclusive" is a third outcome that authorises nothing. Rationale: three runs that disagree, or instrumentation that misses most of the work, are not evidence for stop or go. Date/Author: 2026-11-16 / Claude.
  • Decision: An experiment is compared with its switch-off build in the same session and kept only when the two sides do not overlap. Rationale: a baseline from another day carries device drift, and three runs cannot resolve small differences. Date/Author: 2026-11-16 / Claude.
  • Decision: The first-frame guard runs from the lease request, not from surface attach. Rationale: otherwise moving delay before attach would look like an improvement. Date/Author: 2026-11-16 / Claude.
  • Decision: Numeric thresholds are fixed before the phone session and can be changed by the user until then. Rationale: a rule written after the data fits the data. Date/Author: 2026-11-16 / Claude.
  • Decision: The Viewer's stop of feed previews gets one section, TheoriaPreviewStopForViewer, inside FeedPreviewPlayerPool.stopForViewer. It replaces the per-player cool section there, and the call at ViewerVideoPlayerPool.kt :42 gets no second section, because that call reaches only stopForViewer. Consequence: the mixed journey's previewPlayerCoolCount no longer counts stops for the Viewer, which matches its README meaning. A lower count cannot trip benchmark_results.py compare, which flags only values above the stored limit. Date/Author: 2026-11-16 / Claude.
  • Decision: Surface sections are emitted only when playerView.player changes. Skipping a same-player assignment changes no behaviour, because Media3 1.11.1 PlayerView.setPlayer returns at once when given the player it holds (checked in its bytecode). TheoriaPreviewSurfaceAttach covers replacements as well as first attaches; a replacement includes the blocking detach of the old player. Date/Author: 2026-11-16 / Claude.
  • Decision: The card adding and removing its listener, and reading the ready duration, sit in TheoriaPreviewListenerBind (commands and events), so no player call is left untraced and the existing prepare section keeps its extent. Date/Author: 2026-11-16 / Claude.
  • Decision: An abandoned presentation closes its asynchronous slices inside a TheoriaPreviewAbandoned section on the main thread, and the script treats a slice whose end falls inside that section as abandoned. Rationale: Android's async trace API carries no extra data and trace processor does not expose the cookie, so containment is the exact link. Date/Author: 2026-11-16 / Claude.
  • Decision: The attach-to-first-frame slice starts at the presentation's first surface attach and is not restarted by a later re-attach. Rationale: it is diagnostic only, and one slice per presentation keeps the async slice pairing simple. Date/Author: 2026-11-16 / Claude.
  • Decision: Frames janky only for reasons other than an app deadline miss, "Unknown Jank" and "Dropped Frame" included, are counted as "other jank". Date/Author: 2026-11-16 / Claude.
  • Decision: Phase 2's stop and go use the unsampled section measures only. The sampled check is time against time: inconclusive when the untraced Media3 estimate inside late frames (samples × sampling period) exceeds the section self time inside late frames of the same run, with a proposed ratio of 1. Media3 samples taken while a Viewer player is active are not counted. Rationale: the first rule compared Media3 samples inside sections with those outside. Sampling cannot see blocked waits, and "inside" left out non-Media3 work, so the rule was inconclusive by construction. On the emulator the old measure read 83.6% outside; the new one reads 232 ms untraced against 975.5 ms traced (24%). The section categories still choose the option, and the plan states which options can reach the untraced work. Date/Author: 2026-11-16 / Claude.
  • Decision (the user's, 2026-11-16, before any phone data was read): the Phase 2 thresholds stand as written. Stop: late rate under 1% in both scenarios, or player share under 30% in both. Inconclusive: the three runs' late rates differ by more than 2×, or the sampled run's untraced estimate inside late frames exceeds that run's section self time there; on that outcome the report names what is missing and the smallest instrumentation that would resolve it, with its cost, and a round of it may be authorised where warranted. Go: the experiment is chosen by the data (construction to A, surface to B). The Phase 3 keep rule stands as written. Separately, turning off platform diagnostics for preview players is authorised as its own switch, measured separately by another thread. Date/Author: 2026-11-16 / the user, recorded by Claude.
  • Decision: verify_performance_device.sh requires only that ANDROID_SERIAL is connected, authorized and physical, instead of exactly one connected device. Rationale: every device command in it uses adb -s, and the one-device rule blocked phone batches whenever the shared emulator was up. The debug lane keeps its one-device rule, which protects the personal phone. Approved by the user. Date/Author: 2026-11-16 / Claude.
  • Decision: A sampled frame resolves to the outer method of each R8 line range it covers, and counts as Media3 only when every candidate is Media3. Rationale: sampled frames carry no line number, and a merged class would otherwise put app code into the Media3 count. The estimate therefore leans low. Date/Author: 2026-11-16 / Claude.

Outcomes And Retrospective

Phase 1: every main-thread call into a preview player is traced, and the script reads the default macrobenchmark capture and a sampled capture. Met: the unit test, per-presentation first-frame times, the abandoned count, the hand check, and named Media3 frames with the outside-sections estimate. Partly met: the unsampled mixed-journey capture showed 11 of the 16 sections. Interactive captures added the Viewer, idle-cooling, release and hand-off paths, for 15 of 16; TheoriaPreviewPlayerRebind never fired in any capture. Not met on the emulator: the existing autoplay scenarios fail there on the base commit as well. No performance conclusion is drawn from emulator numbers.

Idempotence And Recovery

Phase 1 adds trace sections, two slices and a script, and changes no behaviour; each part can be reverted alone. Experiments in Phase 3 sit behind a build-time switch and are removed if not kept. Phone traces and benchmark outputs are acceptance evidence: keep every iteration until the decision is recorded and the user agrees they can go.
